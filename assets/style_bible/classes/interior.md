## Style
The inside of the crew's modern, utilitarian near-future research sub: a well-kept working tool, overbuilt rather than old, in the manner of an excavator cab, a research submersible or a modern wheelhouse. Matte, muted powder-coated grey and aluminium surfaces with safety-orange accents. Welded and bolted structure, machined frames, ribs and conduit shown honestly. Guarded toggles, rocker switches and rotary selectors in clean panel layouts, LED indicator lamps and backlit modern gauge faces, engraved label plates as shapes only. Braided cable looms, modern connectors, hex bolts. Hidden cove lighting along the tops of the walls with occasional point sources, holographic map displays, and a glass dome over the pilot. Wear is scuffs, worn paint on edges and walkways and scratched plastic, never corrosion.

## Avoid
- WW2 or antique submarine look: riveted plates, brass, bare rust, valve hand-wheels, rows of antique dials in brass bezels
- cloth-wrapped wiring, exposed bare pipework as the main texture, caged incandescent bulkhead lamps
- stencilled or legible lettering
- brutalist concrete massing, which belongs to the stations
- sleek sci-fi gloss, cyberpunk neon, magenta-and-cyan lighting
